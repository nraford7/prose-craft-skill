# High-Style Figures — the Intensify Layer

The fourth craft axis. SKILL.md gives three: the **Floor** (correctness and economy), the **Filter** (kill machine-tells), the **Ceiling** (construct varied sentences on purpose). This file adds the **Figure** — named rhetorical schemes of *repetition* and *reversal* that intensify a single anchor line so it lands harder than its content alone would.

**How this differs from the Ceiling.** The Ceiling (Tufte) is *sentence architecture* — where the free modifiers branch, where the subject sits, how the series is built. Figures are a different axis: *patterns of sound and structure at the phrase level.* A cumulative sentence is a Ceiling move; a chiasmus is a Figure. They stack — a figured line is often also a built sentence — but they are chosen separately.

**The governing discipline is unchanged.** A figure is craft only when it is *rationed, placed on purpose, and passes the naming test* (name the effect in a phrase — "chiasmus mirrors the reversal the argument just made"). Scattered, a figure is a tell. A whole essay of antitheses is as dead as a whole essay of labels. This file is the positive catalog; the Filter is the budget that keeps it honest. Read both together.

**Register gate.** Load this file only for savored registers (essay, argument, narrative — see SKILL.md → Register Calibration). Scanned text gets no figures.

## Where a figure earns its place

Figures are spent at the piece's **anchor moments** — and a piece has few:

- The **opening line** and the **closing line**.
- The **turn** — the sentence where everything changes.
- The **thesis**, when it is stated outright.
- **Emotional peaks.**
- For decks: the **cover title**, the **turn title**, and the **last title** — the three a friend repeats back the next day.

**Never** in connective tissue (transitions), factual reporting, data captions, or quoted material. A figure there reads as a tonal swerve — technique showing off over argument.

**The feel test.** A figure should read as *a slight intensification of the prose around it*, never as a register shift. If the reader notices the device before the meaning, cut it.

## Family A — Surprise (setup → break)

All five open an expectation in the first half and break it in the second. The cognitive jolt is the payload. Best at the turn, the closing line, and any sentence doing multi-part argumentative work.

| Figure | What it is | Example | Reach for it when |
|--------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Antithesis** | Two opposed ideas in parallel grammar | "We build the tools; the tools build us." | Weighing two forces; the turn; a closing that balances cost against gain |
| **Chiasmus** | The second half inverts the first's order (ABBA) | "Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country." | Mirroring a reversal the argument just made; the pivot beat |
| **Paraprosdokian** | The second half forces a reread of the first | "The strategy is sound. That is the problem." | A hard turn; puncturing a comfortable assumption |
| **Zeugma** | One verb governs objects across shifting registers | "She lost her keys, her nerve, and the account." | Compressing literal and figurative into one beat; wry emphasis |
| **Antanaclasis** | A word repeats with a different meaning each time | "If we don't hang together, we'll hang separately." | A memorable close; making one word carry two weights |

## Family B — Repetition

Repeat a word or structure on purpose. Repetition builds momentum, signals equivalence, and stacks weight. The device rations hard — see the Filter reconciliation below.

| Figure | What it is | Example | Reach for it when |
|--------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Anaphora** | Same phrase begins successive clauses | "We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds…" | A series of points must build as one rising argument |
| **Epistrophe** | Same phrase ends successive clauses | "…of the people, by the people, for the people." | Different subjects reach the same conclusion and it should land as one |
| **Anadiplosis** | Last word of one clause opens the next | "Fear leads to anger; anger leads to hate." | Showing causation; making a sequence feel inevitable |
| **Tricolon** | Three parallel elements | "Government of the people, by the people, for the people." | A memorable triple, a closing, a claim needing rhythmic weight |
| **Isocolon** | Successive clauses of equal length and structure | "The bigger they come, the harder they fall." | Comparison and contrast; matched phrasing makes the difference stand out |
| **Diacope** | A word repeated with one or two words between | "Bond. James Bond." | Single-point intensity; framing one word with conviction |
| **Epizeuxis** | Same word repeated immediately | "Never, never, never give up." | Maximum insistence on one weighted word — use sparingly |
| **Polyptoton** | Same root, different grammatical form | "The Greeks are strong, and skilful to their strength." | Repetition without flatness — the shifted form advances the thought |

## Reconciliation with the Filter (read this before deploying)

The Filter rations several of these devices *as machine-tells*. Those caps still hold — this file is how you build the **one** instance that earns its place, not a licence to exceed the budget.

- **Anaphora** — Filter bans anaphora past two clauses in a row. So a figured anaphora runs to two, on purpose, at a peak; a third is a tell.
- **Tricolon** — Filter caps rule-of-three at **one per section**. This catalog is the craft for building that one as a real series form (asyndeton / polysyndeton / dropped-final-and — see `constructions.md` → The Series). The cap rations the device; the figure earns the survivor.
- **Antithesis vs. negative parallelism** — the Filter hard-bans the reflexive "not X — it's Y" template (zero per piece, all variants). Antithesis must use positive form: state what the thing *is* as a genuine opposition ("X is Y"), never define it by what it isn't ("X is not A — it's B"). If it collapses into the reflexive "not X" template, it is the tell the Filter kills.
- **Banned outright, no figure exempts them:** the countdown ("Not X. Not Y. Just Z."), the self-answered question ("The X? A Y."), the "-ing" significance tail. A figure that decays into one of these is a tell.

**The naming test governs every instance.** Name the effect in a phrase or cut it — *even when you are under budget.* Naming never raises a cap; it decides which instance survives at the cap and strips the unearned ones below it.

**Theme budget.** A chiasmus or antithesis is a magnet for restating the point. State the thesis outright once at the climax and once at the close — a figured restatement mid-body is still a restatement, and the Filter's fractal-summary ban applies to it.

## Checklist

1. Identify the piece's anchor moments (opening, turn, close, thesis, emotional peaks). There are few.
2. For each, choose **one** figure whose mechanism matches the beat — surprise for a turn, repetition for a build, antithesis for a weighing.
3. Run the **naming test**: name the effect in a phrase. Can't? Cut it.
4. Run the **feel test**: does it read as slight intensification, or as technique showing off? If the latter, cut it.
5. Check the **Filter budget**: anaphora ≤ 2 in a row, one tricolon per section, zero negative-parallelism templates. Figures live inside the caps.
6. Check the **theme budget**: a figured line at the climax and close is earned; a figured restatement mid-body is not.
7. Leave everything else plain. The plainness is what makes the figured moment land.
